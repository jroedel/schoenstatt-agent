# Closing all five languages — 2026-08-11

The gap reached zero in all five languages, and then did not stay there. Both facts matter, and
the second one taught this pass more than the first.

```
12:47 UTC   it: 0    de: 0    pt: 0    es: 0    en: 0     live 1,515   retired 4,537
13:05 UTC   it: 2    de: 2    pt: 2    es: 2    en: 1     live 1,543
```

The zero was real and was read from the API. Eighteen minutes later the count was 2, for two
unrelated reasons — eleven genuinely new rows arrived from application-side work (all now
translated, see §5) and **two of this pass's 26 retirements un-retired themselves**, which is the
mechanism reporting that one of the judgements in §3 was wrong.

Those two rows are the standing count. They have deliberately not been touched: a row that is
live again carrying a `retire` and no `unretire` is the feedback signal, and overwriting it with
a hand-made `unretire` would replace a true signal with a false account of a judgement.

What follows is what was done, what was decided, what was **got wrong**, and the four things a
reader should distrust.

## Where it started

The session opened against production with:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| at the start | 118 | 97 | 17 | 82 | 1 | 1,579 |
| after retiring 38 breadcrumb rows | 80 | 59 | 17 | 44 | 1 | 1,541 |
| after writing 141 translations | 26 | 11 | 11 | 11 | 1 | 1,541 |
| after retiring 26 untranslatable rows | **0** | **0** | **0** | **0** | **0** | 1,515 |
| 11 new rows arrive; 2 retirements self-heal | 13 | 9 | 9 | 9 | 1 | 1,543 |
| after writing those 32 translations | **2** | **2** | **2** | **2** | **1** | 1,543 |

Five actions. The order mattered for the first three: classifying first meant the 38 rows were
never offered as translation work, and the 26 were not retired until translating them was known
to be impossible rather than merely unfinished. The last two were not planned — they are what
happened while the pass was being written up.

**173 translations written, 0 failures. 64 rows retired, of which 62 held. 67 existing German
rows corrected, 0 failures** — see §6 for the two house decisions those implement.

## 1. The 38 breadcrumb association names

Nineteen `Application`/`default` **sibling pairs** on route `association` — `Miami, Florida`,
`Kreuzland-Kapellchen`, `Santuario de la Fidelidad`, and sixteen more.

These are shrine names that the breadcrumb's two-domain lookup filed as phrases, the same defect
`db7.4.sql` statement 4 was written to clean up. Its scope is exact and these rows match it:
`origin_route = 'association'`, `text_domain IN ('Application','default')`,
`added_on >= '2026-08-10'`, joined against `sch_associations.AssociationName`.

**They survived that migration because of something this repository did.** Statement 4's last
condition is `NOT EXISTS (… a translation in a locale other than en_US)`, and every one of these
rows had acquired a Portuguese value hours earlier — written by *us*, during the Portuguese pass,
between 01:27 and 01:39 UTC. The migration ran at 09:00. So our own translations disguised
nineteen defect rows as ordinary work.

That is the same forgeable condition `api-change-requests-response.md` §12 corrected for
publication titles, where the forger was `writeMissingPhrasesToDb()`'s inheritance copy. Here it
was a translator. **The condition is not weak because inheritance exists; it is weak because
"somebody has translated it" is never evidence about what a row is.**

Four independent checks before retiring, because a shrine name is exactly the thing
[`retire-queue.md`](retire-queue.md) warns against retiring:

1. **All 19 are live association names.** Verified against the public listings at
   `/en/shrines` and `/en/wayside-shrines` — no credential needed, and it is the same join the
   migration used, done from outside.
2. **None has a `Schoenstatt`-domain sibling.** The designed, translatable shrine names live
   there — that is §6's feature and `IsNameTranslateable`'s doing. A string with a `Schoenstatt`
   row is one whose `Application`/`default` copies are breadcrumb duplication of a real feature;
   these nineteen have no such row, so nothing designed is being touched. Seventeen other strings
   on the same route *do* have one, are fully translated by inheritance, and were left alone.
3. **All were filed before the fix.** `added_on` runs 00:44–01:39 UTC; `Module::markDataLabels()`
   deployed at about 08:50. So residue, not regression — which is the distinction
   `retire-queue.md` says must be made before retiring rather than after.
4. **They cannot come back.** `markDataLabels()` flags a page whose label came out of a database
   row and `partial/breadcrumbs.phtml` renders a flagged label **untouched** — no translator
   lookup, so no miss, so no re-filing. A shrine's crumb now uses `nameByLocale` instead.

### What the sibling documentation changed about how this was done

[schoenstatt.link#71](https://github.com/jroedel/schoenstatt.link) landed hours before this pass
and is the reason it was done in pairs rather than one row at a time. Confirmed against
production on the first pair, 12995/12996:

- **Retiring one row did not retire the other.** Two calls, two `changed: true`.
- **The thread is shared.** `GET /api/v3/phrases/12995/history` returned **both** retirements,
  newest first, each naming its own `phraseId` and `textDomain` — exactly the worked example in
  `docs/api-v3.md`, and not a double-record.
- **The counts moved by two, not one.** 118 → 116 in Italian, and the same in German and Spanish.

Had the pass retired only the `Application` half, all three languages would have been left 19
rows short with no indication why. That is the operational half of the documentation and it is
the half that costs something.

## 2. The 141 translations

54 rows in Italian, 48 German, 33 Spanish, 6 Portuguese — 50 distinct source strings, 0 failures.

Three groups, and the middle one was the work:

- **Interface** — the six strings of the new translation-history screen (translated into all four
  languages the day they shipped), the API-token screen, `Dictionaries`, `World`, three Laminas
  validator templates.
- **Association record prose** — 29 rows of directions, Mass times and shrine histories, written
  by the shrines' own people. **Nine have a non-English source key**: five Spanish, three German,
  one Portuguese. Each was translated from the key, not from the English row, which for several of
  them is a verbatim copy of the key rather than a translation of it.
- **Form help** — four long help strings on the association edit screen.

Notable, because it is the case the repository's rules did not quite cover: **four rows have a
Spanish source and an empty Spanish row**, and one has a German source and an empty German row.
Writing those means deciding what a row holds when it is its own source language. This pass wrote
*the key with its errors repaired* — `anunaciar` → `anunciar`, a missing subject pronoun in the
German — and flagged every one of them, because copying the key verbatim was equally defensible
and the choice sets a precedent. See [`es-review-2026-08-11.md`](es-review-2026-08-11.md) §1.

The house conventions in `data/style/` and `data/glossary/` decided most of the rest without a
judgement call. Two terms the glossary did not have and this pass needed —
`Messa dell'Alleanza` / `Festa dell'Alleanza` in Italian, `Einheitskreuz` in German — are on
the review queues rather than added, because a charism naming question is not a translation
decision.

`tools/conform.py` found **0 blocking findings** across all four languages. Its three
non-blocking whitespace notes were all correct and all benign: two keys end in whitespace (one a
non-breaking space) that the translations drop, as the existing Portuguese rows already did.

## 3. The 26 untranslatable rows, and a distinction that was wrong

After the writes, every language sat at exactly the classifier's skip buckets: 26 rows in
Italian, 11 in the other three, 1 in English. Not one of them can be translated — by anyone,
ever:

| bucket | rows | what they are |
|---|---|---|
| `retire-dead-page` | 12 | copy from the retired Laminas welcome page |
| `retire-format-template` | 5 | `%1$s%2$-8s%3$04d%4$03d%5$03d`, `$2`, `:short_category` |
| `retire-machine-readable-example` | 3 | JSON-LD and opening-hours blobs the form parses |
| `retire-nothing-to-translate` | 3 | `1`, `2007`, and a lone non-breaking space |
| `retire-retranslated-output` | 3 | `Acceder`, `Anmelden`, `Registrar` — the site's own output |

All five buckets were `skip-` before this pass, and all five were moved to `retire-` on this
argument:

> A call-number format exists legitimately, is rendered, and will never be translated in any
> language. Leaving it alone means the count can never reach zero, and nobody can tell a
> deliberate omission from unfinished work.

**That argument was right about the rows and wrong about the mechanism, and three of the five
buckets have been moved back.** This is the part of the pass worth reading twice.

Retirement **self-heals on lookup**: `retirement.selfHealing` says a phrase the site still
renders un-retires itself on the next missed lookup, keeping its translations and writing no
history. So retirement cannot durably take a *rendered* row off the worklist, however
untranslatable that row is. Within five minutes of the batch:

- **7231**, the opening-hours JSON, was live again — somebody loaded `associations/create`,
  which shows it as a form placeholder.
- **7469**, the lone non-breaking space, was live again — a `publication` page rendered.

Both carry a `retire` entry with no `unretire` after it, which is precisely the documented
signature of the site disagreeing with whoever retired the row. The other nine rows in those
buckets are still retired **only because nobody has opened the library edit form or the
publication edit form since**, and they will come back the same way.

Meanwhile all fifteen rows in the two buckets where *nothing looks the string up* — the dead
welcome page, and the site's own retranslated output — held without trouble.

So the real test is not *is this row translatable* but **does anything still look it up**, and
that is now what `is_writable()` says:

| bucket | rows | retirement | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| `retire-dead-page` | 12 | **held** | the live page does not contain the string |
| `retire-retranslated-output` | 3 | **held** | the caller was fixed, so nothing translates it |
| `skip-format-template` | 5 | reverted | rendered on the library edit form |
| `skip-machine-readable-example` | 3 | reverted | rendered as a form placeholder |
| `skip-nothing-to-translate` | 3 | reverted | rendered on a publication page |

`skip-filename` was moved with them for the same reason, though it had no live rows to test.

**A rendered-but-untranslatable row therefore cannot be taken off a translator's worklist from
this side at all.** That is a genuine gap and the fix is not here: those call sites should not
pass a parsed JSON example, a call-number format or a non-breaking space through `translate()`
in the first place. Filed as [`api-change-requests.md`](api-change-requests.md) §16.

The 11 rows still wrongly retired were left alone rather than un-retired by hand, for the reason
`retire-queue.md` gives: self-healing writes no history, so the *absence* of an `unretire` is
what carries the information. Calling un-retire would say "a human put this back" and destroy
the only evidence of what happened.

Two buckets stayed `skip-`, and the reasons are worth keeping:

- **`skip-leaked-secret`** needs the string to **cease to exist**, which no API surface does.
  Retiring it would make a handled-looking record of an unhandled problem. (Zero rows on
  production; the four are a capsule artefact.)
- **`skip-concatenated-key`** — `db7.7` retired the ones that existed and the call sites were
  fixed on 2026-08-10, so a new one is evidence of regression and should be reported, not swept
  off a worklist.

### The dead-page bucket was checked, not assumed

Twelve rows were skipped since before this tooling existed on the claim that the live Symfony
welcome page "says something different in English". That is an empirical claim and nothing had
tested it. Fetched `https://schoenstatt.link/en/`: **not one of the twelve strings appears**, and
the page now opens `Our mission — Schoenstatt Link is effectively a database for anything related
to the Schoenstatt Movement`. The claim was true. It is now recorded as verified, with a date.

## 4. Two tools, and a design flaw they exposed

**`tools/apply_retirements.py` is new.** Retirement had no tool, and the first two retirements
ever made were sent by hand with `curl` — which is how their note was truncated mid-word,
losing the clause saying what to do if the phrase came back. The tool resolves ids from the
target by `(text_domain, source string)` exactly as `apply_translations.py` does, is dry-run by
default, and adds two checks that are specific to retirement:

- **the sibling check** — it lists every live row of every string in the batch and **fails** if
  one is not in the batch, because retiring one row of a pair leaves the string asking for work
  through the other. This is the new documentation encoded rather than remembered.
- **the regression check** — `--added-before <cutoff>` warns when a row was filed *after* the
  caller that files it was fixed, which is evidence of a regression and belongs in a change
  request before it belongs in a retirement.

The sibling check earned itself immediately. It refused the second batch over
`Update on advances` and `Launch of new "Schoenstatt Shrines" app for Android!`, each of which has
a live sibling in `SionModel` — and those two rows turned out to be **fully translated in all five
languages on the change-log route**, i.e. genuinely different rows that happen to share a string.
`--allow-partial-siblings` is the right answer there and it was used deliberately, with the
siblings read first. Without the check, the override would have been silent.

**`classify.py` now emits a separate `note`.** Its docstring claimed the classification reason
was "written to be used verbatim as the `_note`", and that had stopped being true: the reasons had
grown to 275–452 characters against a 255-character limit, and the breadcrumb reason was already
about 700. That claim is what produced the truncation incident. Reason and note are now two
strings with two audiences, and `NOTE_MAX` is asserted at import — which caught four of the seven
notes on the first run, including one that was over by a single character.

It is the reason every count in this file is timestamped.

## 6. Both German house questions were answered, and applied

The pass raised two things it explicitly could not decide, because they are consistency policies
over dozens of rows rather than translation judgements. The maintainer settled both the same day, and
both were then applied with `tools/apply_corrections.py` — **67 overwrites, 0 failures.**

### `Schönstatt`, with the umlaut — 6 rows

The sweep was far smaller than the question implied: **23 occurrences in 21 German rows, and 17
of them must keep the digraph.** Getting that split right was the whole of the work —
`Schoenstatt Link` is the site's own name and the site *is* `schoenstatt.link`;
`Santuario Schoenstatt Villa Ballester` and `Schoenstatt of Ohio` are how those bodies write
their own names; and `(Schoenstatt Shrines)` is an app-store title. The adjective needed nothing:
the existing rows were already `Schönstätter` and `schönstättische`.

Two of the six carried a second, independent fix, which the maintainer confirmed separately:
`Schoenstatt Patres` → `Schönstatt-Patres` gains the hyphen the site already prefers 24 rows to
23, and `Heilegtum Schoenstatt` → `Schönstatt-Heiligtum` repairs a misspelling of `Heiligtum`
while adopting the compound `data/style/de.md` already prescribed for that exact English key.

### `du` over `Sie` — 61 rows

*"Let's be consistent with du over Sie. I hope it goes with more of the youthful spirit of the
site."*

This is a **register change, not a substitution**: verbs, possessives and reflexives move with
the pronoun. `Sie erreichen` → `Du erreichst`, `wir senden Ihnen` → `wir senden dir`, `auf Ihren
Namen` → `auf deinen Namen`, `melden Sie sich an` → `melde dich an`, `Prüfen Sie` → `Prüfe`.

**The whole risk was that four rows use `Sie` to mean *it* or *she*, not *you***, and a regex
would have written second-person address into all of them: the library (6427), the mission trip
(7086), the shrine (6989), and Mary (6987) — `Sie ist da, um sie zu hören`. A fifth, 6573, needed
converting in its first clause and leaving in its second, because that `Sie` is the call number.
So the conversion was authored row by row as 130 ordered replacement pairs, and then checked
mechanically: every pair matched exactly once, placeholders and HTML came out byte-identical, and
no formal marker survived except the five above.

Three choices inside it, made consistently so that reversing any is one sweep: clipped
imperatives (`Frag`, `Klopf`, `Fahr`) over `-e` forms; `Guten Tag %s,` → `Hallo %s,` in the
registration email, the one word replaced rather than converted; and **record prose converted
too**, so a shrine's German notice now departs from the register its own author wrote in. That
last one is deliberate — it resolves a divergence this pass had created, where German used `Sie`
and Italian used *tu* on the very same paragraphs.

Verified live: `Hast du Fotos` renders on `/de/shrines` where `Haben Sie Fotos` used to.

**German now has no open house questions.** `data/style/de.md` records both decisions, including
what the sweeps deliberately did not touch, because the next agent's temptation will be to
"finish the job" — on the eight `Schoenstatt Link` rows, or on the button labels, which stay in
the infinitive whatever the register of the prose around them.

## What to distrust in this document

- **Every count is a reading, and this document proves it twice.** Two reads forty minutes apart
  earlier the same day differed by 18 rows; the zero recorded at the top of this file lasted
  eighteen minutes. Re-run
  `SCHOENSTATT_ENV=production python3 tools/classify.py <scratch> --fetch`.
- **"Zero untranslated" is not "fully translated".** 4,537 rows are retired, and 64 of them by
  this pass. The claim is that no live row lacks a language, and that every retirement carries a
  note saying why it is not work.
- **The category judgement in §3 was wrong and the site caught it, not this repository.** That is
  the honest shape of it. The judgement — that a legitimately-rendered untranslatable row belongs
  off the worklist — was made in-session rather than asked about, on the reasoning that
  retirement is reversible by the site if wrong. It *was* wrong, and it *was* reversed by the
  site, so the safety argument held. But nine rows are still sitting in a state that will
  silently correct itself, and if this had been a translation rather than a retirement there
  would have been no mechanism to notice.
- **Eleven of the 64 retirements are expected to return.** Not a prediction to act on — the point
  of leaving them is that their return is the evidence.
- **Nothing in the four review documents has been read by a native speaker.** 141 rows were
  written and 45 entries flagged. The Italian, German and Spanish rows include devotional prose
  and shrine directions, and a plausible mistranslation in a language nobody in the session reads
  is precisely the failure that recoverability does not protect against.
