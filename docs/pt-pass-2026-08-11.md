# Closing the Portuguese gap, 2026-08-11

Portuguese went from **1,183 to 1,530** translated phrases on production, and its
translatable gap is now **zero**. It is the best-covered language after English, ahead of
Spanish (1,464), German (1,449) and Italian (1,429).

344 rows written in six batches, 0 failed. 21 corrections applied alongside them, each with a
`_note` — see [`corrections-2026-08-11.md`](corrections-2026-08-11.md).

| batch | beat | rows | what it was |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `association` | 62 | shrine directions, opening times, the wayside-shrine screens |
| 2 | `account` | 46 | sign-in, registration, people, roles, API tokens |
| 3 | `misc` + `library3` | 62 | role names, music metadata, the Kentenich era categories |
| 4 | `library2` | 49 | the publication show and edit screens |

## The gap was half the size it looked, and the other half was a defect

The pass began with a Portuguese gap of **576 distinct source strings** and ended having
translated **309**. The difference was not scope creep in reverse — it was two classifier
rules that had to be written first, and one that had to be undone.

### 509 publication and composition titles were never work

The single biggest finding of the pass, and it was not about Portuguese at all. 234 of the
first worklist's units were on route `publication` and they were **book titles** —
`Apologia pro vita mea`, `Das Ehe-Ideal`, `A házaspárok útján`, `Svatynka pramen zivota`.
Counted across the corpus: 427 of the 436 rows on `publication` and 82 of the 87 on
`composition`, **509 rows**.

They arrive because the show pages hand the record's own title to `headTitle()`, which
translates whatever it holds. This is §7's defect — the text corpus — on two pages that were
missed, and it was invisible until the discovery repair landed the same day. Filed as
[`api-change-requests.md`](api-change-requests.md) §12; excluded meanwhile as
`skip-publication-title`.

The rule's signature is exact rather than approximate: the English row is a verbatim copy of
the key **and** no other language has ever translated it. On `publication` the nine rows that
fail the second test are precisely the nine genuine interface strings — `Data source`,
`Date published`, `Leave a comment`, `Total views`, `Copy into main corpus` and the rest — all
of which were translated in batches 4 and 6. Deliberately not applied outside those two
routes: elsewhere the same signature just means "nobody has translated this yet", and 24 rows
on `association` alone would have been wrongly skipped.

This also corrected the working contract. `CLAUDE.md` had said a publication's title "never
reaches a translator this way". That was true only while discovery was broken.

### 33 shrine descriptions were work, and had been wrongly excluded

The opposite error, and the more embarrassing one. `classify.py`'s `skip-record-content`
bucket excluded 37 rows of association free text on the theory that a translation goes stale
when a moderator edits the record. The maintainers' reply §6 establishes that
`sch_associations.PublicNotes` is run through the translator **on purpose** — it is how an
Italian-speaking pilgrim gets a shrine's directions in Italian — and that nothing was ever
stale, because a phrase's identity *is* its text.

For a repository whose stated purpose is making the shrines easier to find and visit, those
were the most valuable rows in the backlog, and this tooling was excluding them. The bucket is
rebuilt: the line is **machine-readable versus prose**, not interface versus content. Three
rows stayed excluded as `skip-machine-readable-example` (an opening-hours JSON blob, a JSON-LD
event skeleton, a call-number format — the application parses those keys). The remaining 33
were translated in batch 1, including the directions to Vienna, Mendoza, Cambrai, Garín, San
Antonio and Monterrey, and the Ballester shrine's mass and adoration times.

### A stop list that only knew English

The beat files list each beat's recurrent terms as the decisions to settle first. On the
association beat that list opened with `que`, `por`, `del`, `der`, `die`, `dem` and `des` —
because the shrine descriptions on those routes were typed by moderators in Spanish and
German, and the stop list held English function words only. **A source string is not
guaranteed to be English** is one of this project's standing facts, and the tooling had not
absorbed it. Fixed; cross-beat noise fell from 14 terms to 6.

## What the site's own data settled

**`ermida` is the Portuguese for a wayside shrine, and this is now attested.** The glossary
had carried `Ermida de Schoenstatt` as an open question on a single lowercase corpus
occurrence. Phrase 6970 — the public notes for the wayside shrine at Grada, Vila Nova de
Monsarros (Anadia) — has a **Portuguese source string**, written by or for the parish and
quoting the bishop of Aveiro, and it uses `ermida` throughout for exactly this structure:
`está a nascer uma ermida dedicada a Mãe, Rainha e Vencedora Três Vezes Admirável de
Schoenstatt`, the heading `BÊNÇÃO DA ERMIDA`, and `Ermida de Grada (Anadia)`. Native usage
from the Portuguese movement itself, which the books could not supply.

`Wayside shrines` is therefore `Ermidas`, beside es `Ermitas`, de `Bildstöcke`, it `Edicole`.

**Shrine names follow one rule, and the site had already made it.** `Familia Patris Heiligtum,
Berg Sion, Horw` was rendered `Santuário Família do Pai, Berg Sion, Horw` — descriptive part
translated, toponym kept. So `Santuario de la Fidelidad` became `Santuário da Fidelidade` and
`Milwaukee, Wisconsin` stayed as it was. Twenty toponyms were written verbatim on that basis.

**The language dictionary is eleven decisions, not forty-four rows.** The site stores each
language's name as a phrase in whatever language somebody typed it, so `Alemán`, `Alemão`,
`Deutsch` and `Tedesco` are four rows and one answer. Drafted as a map from the Portuguese name
to its aliases, which is the shape the decision actually has.

## Flagged for a Portuguese speaker

Eleven judgement calls, written with what was chosen, the alternative, and why. The ones worth
a second opinion most:

- **`Pfr.` → `Pe.`** `Pfarrer` is a parish priest. es uses `Pbro.` (Presbítero), it `Don`.
  `Pe.` is the Brazilian abbreviation for Padre; `Pároco` is the exact office rather than the
  courtesy title.
- **`presidio general` → `presídio geral`** in a shrine note. In Schoenstatt this is the
  General Presidium; in ordinary Portuguese `presídio` means a prison. Almost certainly wants
  the movement's own term.
- **`Kreuzland-Kapellchen` → `Capelinha de Kreuzland`.** Follows the shrine-name rule, but a
  German speaker should confirm `Kapellchen` is the diminutive rather than part of a proper
  name.
- **`Józefow - Syjon` kept verbatim.** `Syjon` is Polish for Zion; the site left `Berg Sion`
  and `Sião Jaraguá` alone, so this was left too.
- **`AudiobookFormat`, `EBook`, `Hardcover`, `Paperback`** written as ordinary words
  (`Audiolivro`, `Livro eletrônico`, `Capa dura`, `Brochura`). These keys are schema.org enum
  names; es mimics the CamelCase, de and it read them as words. Three languages disagree.
- **`association` → `organização`** on the roles screens, where it means an organisation record
  of any kind rather than a Schoenstatt community. es and de both used the community word.
- **`Branch moderator` → `Assessor de ramo`**, and the older paired row corrected to match.
- **`Koblenz` → `Coblença`**, matching es/it `Coblenza`; some Brazilian sources keep `Koblenz`.

## Defects found in other languages while translating

Reading a beat's siblings for house convention turns up their errors as a side effect. Five
Spanish rows were corrected in the same session, all of them collapsed pairs or wrong senses:

| phrase | was | now |
|---|---|---|
| `Lyricist(s)` | `Compositor(es)` — identical to `Composer(s)` | `Letrista(s)` |
| `Subedition(s)` | `Otras ediciones` — identical to `Other editions` | `Subedición(es)` |
| `Main publication (…)` | `Publicación actual` — *current*, not main | `Publicación principal (…)` |
| `Show editions separately?` | `¿Mostrar ediciones obsoletas?` | `¿Mostrar ediciones por separado?` |

The last was one of the six items the maintainers' reply §9 assigned to this repository. Three
of those six are now done; `Ladies of Schoenstatt` and `Madrugadores of %s` remain open because
they turn on what a community is called, and `The form submitted did not originate from the
expected site` needs the en and es rows judged together.

Two collapsed pairs in one session suggests looking for more of them deliberately: a check for
**two different source strings sharing one translation within a text domain** would find that
class mechanically, and does not exist yet.

but ~756 of each is the skip list. Re-run `classify.py --fetch` before believing any of them.
The real remaining work is smaller than it looks, and the same two classifier rules that
halved the Portuguese gap apply to all three.
