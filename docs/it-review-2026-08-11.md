# Italian review — the 65 rows written on 2026-08-11

The Italian gap reached **0 untranslated** on production at 12:47 UTC, out of 1,515 live rows,
and then moved again — eleven new rows arrived at 12:49 and were translated too (§7), and two
retirements un-retired themselves. See [`close-2026-08-11.md`](close-2026-08-11.md) for why the
count is 2 rather than 0 and why those two were left alone.

This document is the 19 of that day's 65 Italian writes where a native Italian speaker would
settle something this repository could only decide.

It is deliberately shorter than
[`it-review-queue.md`](it-review-queue.md), which holds the 100 entries from the August 10th
pass and is still open. The two do not overlap.

**Every phrase id below is a production id.** The capsule uses a different id for some of
the same phrases — resolve by source string, never by carrying an id across.

**A correction is now recoverable.** Since the 2026-08-11 deploy,
`GET /api/v3/phrases/{id}/history` gives back the exact previous text, so replacing anything
here is a normal write and the version it replaces is not lost. That was not true of the
August 10th queue and is the one thing that has genuinely changed for a reviewer.

**How to answer.** If the Italian is right, say so — that is the most useful answer and it
retires the entry. If it is wrong, the replacement goes in as an ordinary write against the
phrase id, with a `_note` saying what was wrong.

The `es` / `pt` / `de` values quoted are what the site shows in those languages. They are
evidence of **house convention**, not of correct Italian, and where they disagree with each
other that disagreement is usually why the entry is here.

## 1. Two words the whole pass leans on

### 1.1 `edicola` for a wayside shrine — used in five records

*phrase ids 6987, 6988, 6971, 7453, 6970*

`data/glossary/it.yaml` settles this on the movement's own Italian authority: the page on the
network of shrines has a section headed `Le edicole di Schoenstatt` and glosses it
`Le cappelle lungo la strada (edicole)`. This pass is the first time the word has been used
in **running prose** rather than in a label, five times, in descriptions written by the
shrines' own people.

The question for a reviewer is not whether `edicola` is the right term — the movement's page
settles that — but whether it carries the register these paragraphs have. They are devotional
writing: *"un pezzetto di terra molto vicino al cielo"*. In ordinary Italian `edicola` is also
a newsstand, and this is where that collision would show if it is going to.

es `Ermita`, pt `Ermida`, de `Bildstock` — a hermitage, a hermitage and a wayside pillar.
Three different objects, so the siblings cannot help here.

### 1.2 `Messa dell'Alleanza` and `Festa dell'Alleanza`

*phrase ids 6980, 6993, 7285*

The glossary has `Alleanza d'Amore` for the Covenant of Love, on the movement's Italian pages.
It does not have the two derived names, and this pass needed both — a Mass held on the 18th and
the feast itself. Written as `Messa dell'Alleanza` and `Festa dell'Alleanza`, following the
German row on 6980, which reads `Bündnismesse`.

If the Italian movement says `Messa del Patto` or keeps `Alleanza d'Amore` in full, three rows
change and the glossary gains an entry.

## 2. Where the source string is not English

Nine of the 54 rows have a non-English key. Two of them raise the same question.

### 2.1 `La Ermita de la Unión` — name translated or kept?

*phrase id 7453 · `Schoenstatt` · route `association-edit` · source: Spanish*

**Written:** `L'Ermita de la Unión è un terreno in cui noi schoenstattiani abbiamo uno spazio
per pregare, meditare e condividere. …  Su Waze l'edicola si trova come: "La Ermita. Mov.
Schoenstatt. Dulce Nombre, La Unión."`

The shrine's **name** is kept in Spanish while the generic noun is translated (`edicola`).
The reason is the last sentence: it tells the reader to search Waze for a literal Spanish
string, and translating the name in the first sentence while quoting the Spanish in the last
reads oddly.

**Portuguese did the opposite** — `A Ermida da União`. One of the two is house style and this
repository cannot tell which. The answer applies to every wayside-shrine name in a description,
not only this one.

### 2.2 `Fue bendecida por el P. Jorge Faliszeck…` — `capitale di grazie` lowercase

*phrase id 6989 · `Schoenstatt` · route `associations/association` · source: Spanish*

**Written:** `È stata benedetta da P. Jorge Faliszeck il 30 maggio 1998; nel luogo è stata
sepolta, come capitale di grazie, una corona di 62 fiori che rappresentavano le 62 missionarie
presenti in quel periodo.`

The glossary gives `Capitale di Grazie` capitalized, as a charism term. Here it is lowercase,
because the Spanish source has it lowercase inside a subordinate clause and it is being used
descriptively rather than named. 6988 in the same pass capitalizes it, because there the
Spanish does (`el Capital de Gracias`). Following the source's own casing per row is a rule; it
may be the wrong one, in which case both rows want the capitalized form.

Note the key ends with a non-breaking space, which the Italian drops — as the Portuguese row
already did.

### 2.3 The German shrine description that also needs German

*phrase ids 9701, 9702 · `Schoenstatt` · route `association-edit` · source: German*

**Written (it):** `Il santuario è stato chiuso. L'altare del santuario è stato donato al
Movimento di Schoenstatt ungherese per un futuro santuario a Budapest. Attualmente si trova
nella Casa della Gioventù di Schoenstatt a Budapest.`

`Casa della Gioventù di Schoenstatt` renders de `Haus der Schönstatt-Jugend`. The glossary has
the two youth **branches** (`Gioventù Femminile`, `Gioventù Maschile`) but not the house, and
whether an Italian reader would recognise that name for a building in Budapest is a question
for somebody who knows the Hungarian movement.

**These two rows are the same description one grammar fix apart** — `ein zukünftigen` against
`ein zukünftiges`. Both were translated rather than one retired, because retiring the wrong
half would remove a live description. See the pass document; one of them is almost certainly
superseded and the retirement wants a human who can look at the record.

## 3. Register in long record prose

### 3.1 `Caro Pellegrino` and *tu* in a notice addressed to visitors

*phrase ids 7285, 7468 · `Schoenstatt` · source: Spanish*

**Written (7468):** `Caro Pellegrino, questo luogo di accoglienza ti attende. Per favore
annuncia la tua visita in anticipo, così da poterti ricevere e offrirti un'accoglienza
calorosa e adeguata, scrivendo alla pagina Facebook. …`

`data/style/it.md` establishes *tu* as the house voice for the **interface**, on the evidence
of es `Ayúdanos` and de `Nimm Kontakt zu uns auf`. This is not interface text: it is a shrine
in Argentina addressing pilgrims, and its Spanish is emphatically formal — `lo espera`,
`anticipe su visita`, `brindarle`. Portuguese kept that formality (`o espera`, `avise`).

Italian here follows the **house voice** rather than the source's register, which is a choice
and not obviously the right one. If a shrine's own notice should keep the *Lei* its author
used, two rows change and the style guide gains a sentence distinguishing interface from
record content.

### 3.2 `a 10 isolati` for `a 10 cuadras`

*phrase ids 7285, 7468*

Argentine *cuadra* is a city block. `isolato` is the standard Italian word and is what an
Italian pilgrim reading a walking distance would expect; `quadra` would be a borrowing. Worth
one confirmation because it appears in both Villa Ballester rows.

### 3.3 `sorelle dell'adorazione` lowercase in prose

*phrase id 7198 · `Schoenstatt` · route `association-edit` · source: English*

**Written:** `… Basta chiedere direttamente alle sorelle dell'adorazione. In genere trovano il
tempo di mostrare il loro santuario — soprattutto a un pellegrino ;-). …`

The community's own name row (14180) is `Sorelle dell'Adorazione`, capitalized. Here it is
lowercase because the English is (`the adoration sisters`) and the sentence refers to the
people rather than naming the institute. The winking emoticon is kept, as the other languages
kept it.

## 4. The new translation-history screen

Six strings shipped with the history feature on 2026-08-11 and were translated into all four
languages the same day. Two of the six are structural choices, made identically in it/de/es/pt
so that a reviewer changing one changes all four.

### 4.1 `Replaced` → `Sostituito il`

*phrase id 17228 · `JTranslate` · route `jtranslate/phrase/edit`*

`Replaced` is a `<th>` over a column of **timestamps** — confirmed by reading
`module/JTranslate/view/j-translate/j-translate/edit.phtml`, where it sits beside `Language`,
`Previous text` and `Note`. So all four languages render it as *replaced on* rather than a bare
participle: it `Sostituito il`, de `Ersetzt am`, es `Reemplazado el`, pt `Substituído em`.

The English is bare. If that column ever shows something other than a date, all four are wrong.

### 4.2 `%s previous version(s) — …` → the count moved behind a colon

*phrase id 17221 · `JTranslate` · route `jtranslate`*

**Written:** `Versioni precedenti: %s — una traduzione qui è già stata sostituita`

The English hedges the plural with `version(s)`. `%s` is a count and is always at least 1,
because the icon only renders when the count is positive — so a literal Italian rendering
would produce `1 versioni precedenti`. Moving the count behind a colon avoids the agreement
problem without the `(s)` hedge. It is a tooltip, so there is no width constraint.

### 4.3 `Note` → `Nota`

*phrase id 17227*

`Nota` is the obvious word and probably right. Flagged only because the column holds a
translator's *justification for a change*, not a general annotation, and if Italian wants
`Motivazione` there this is the moment to say so.

## 5. Interface strings with one thing to check each

### 5.1 `Login` → `Accedi`

*phrase id 12911 · `JUser` · route `zfcuser/login`*

es `Acceder`, pt `Entrar`, de `Anmelden` — all three render the action, not the noun, so
`Accedi` follows the style guide's imperative rule and matches `Sign in` → `Accedi` already
in the site chrome. If this particular string is a page **heading** rather than the button,
Italian wants `Accesso`.

### 5.2 `Invalid type given. String expected` → `Tipo non valido. Era prevista una stringa`

*phrase id 13574 · `default` · route `association`*

A Laminas validator template, translated as a template now that the 2026-08-10 fix stopped the
site translating finished messages. `stringa` translates the technical term; **Portuguese kept
the English `string`**, so the four languages now split on whether this vocabulary is
translated. One decision settles all of them.

The house rendering for the rest of this validator family came from phrase 7212 — `Il valore
inserito non sembra un nome di rete locale valido` — which is why 12919 reads `Il valore
inserito non sembra un indirizzo IP valido` and not something invented.

### 5.3 `%sREST API%s` → `%sAPI REST%s`

*phrase ids 13453, 13454 · `Schoenstatt` and `default` · route `shrines.locale`*

The two `%s` are the opening and closing `<a>`, so the words between them are the **link
text** and their order changes with the language. Italian and Spanish put the noun first
(`API REST`); German hyphenates (`REST-API`). Portuguese already did the same.

Worth knowing about this row: **the English key contains two errors** — `any apps or websites
who which to receive`, where `which` is *wish* and `who` should be *that*. All three new rows
translate the intended meaning. The key cannot be edited from here; the fix is where the
phrase is generated.

### 7.1 `Taken off the worklist` → `Rimossa dalla lista di lavoro`

*phrase id 17230 · `JTranslate` · route `jtranslate/phrase/edit`*

The retirement row's label in the phrase history table, paired with `Put back on the worklist`

### 7.2 `How precisely the foundation date is known` and its three options

*phrase ids 17247, 17248, 17249, 17250 · `Schoenstatt` · route `associations/create`*

**Written:** `Con quale precisione si conosce la data di fondazione`, with
`Giorno esatto` / `Solo mese e anno` / `Solo anno`.

Worth one look because **four sibling labels exist on the person form** — birth, ordination,
episcopal ordination and death date — and none of them is translated yet. Whatever is settled here
sets the pattern for five fields, not one. The three options were kept grammatically parallel
within each language, which is why it is `Solo anno` rather than `Solo l'anno`.

### 7.3 `Database edits` → `Modifiche alla base di dati`

*phrase ids 17240, 17234 · `default` and `SionModel` · route `sion-model/view-changes.locale`*

`base di dati` rather than `database`, following the site's own existing Italian — the welcome text
reads `la base di dati collaborativa dei Santuari di Schoenstatt`. Portuguese has this singular
(`Edição da base de dados`) and Spanish plural; Italian follows the English plural.

One row needed no judgement at all and is recorded only so it is not re-examined:
`Invalid call number format for library` (17254) is phrase 6862 —
`Invalid call number format for collection`, already in all four languages — with one noun changed,
